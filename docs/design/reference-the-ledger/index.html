Mage Arena · design variant 1 · The Ledger

A camp that moves every night by rules you can test, and an arena where a mage reads the enemy and drinks its spells.

If this ships, the owner gets a complete, offline, living-room game on the Fire TV stack that already works: a real-time arena that feels like spellcraft within three minutes, and a camp of 21 characters whose lives change overnight without a single required call to a language model, so the world can never be broken by one.

0language-model calls needed to play. The optional live voice costs about 0.002 USD a night (placeholder prices), about 0.08 USD a 42-day season.
1,000headless 42-day seasons run per build: determinism, coverage of every situation state, and proof that the player's choices change the world.
Wave 1is a mage moving and shooting on the real Stick with a phone. The sister project's stack (pure-JVM core, libGDX, phone controller) is reused, not reinvented.

The bet

Code decides what happened. The language model only says it.

The owner asked for an LLM-driven daily reconciliation. This variant keeps the owner's goal (a living, sensible social world) and moves the authority: a deterministic social simulation decides every character's day, and the model's job is the voice of the camp, written mostly at build time.

The model never decides who betrays whom. It only decides how the camp tells it.A model that decides outcomes must be right every night for 42 nights, offline, cheaply and in character. A model that phrases outcomes already decided only has to be readable, and when it fails a template takes its place without the player noticing.

The axes on which the two variants differ

Variant 2 (Fourteen Nights) is the honest alternative: the model directs the camp every night and the camp is a time loop. Same owner concept, same four mains, different answers.

The wow, minute by minute

The first ten minutes: both halves, twice

The player fights before they talk, talks before they sleep, and wakes to a camp that moved. Two arena moments and one full camp day fit inside ten minutes.

Timeline of the opening (authored durations, measured in W7 with a stopwatch on the Stick; the owner certifies whether it lands).

What the arena half must deliver by minute 4

A raider's shaman throws a slow green curse. The game says nothing; the curse glows, a ring shrinks under the mage. The player holds the absorb button, the curse splashes. The second one they time: a white ring flashes, a bell, mana flows back, and a rune on the collar cracks open early. That is the moment the player understands they are a mage, not a gunner.

What the camp half must deliver by minute 8

On day 1 a starving girl at the Commons asks for bread. Most players walk past. At dawn the board says: "Fenna sleeps in the Stone Tent now. She did not look at you when she passed." and "A rumour about you: you ran at the ford." Nobody scripted those for this player; the night computed them.

Loop 1 of 4

The camp day loop

Three slots a day (morning, afternoon, evening), nine places, one shared menu of 14 activities that every character chooses from. Magic does not work inside the camp, so every rivalry is social: bread, rumours, theft, persuasion, unarmed bouts in the Pit.

One day. Blue boxes are code; the purple box is the only place a language model may appear, and it writes words, not state.

One week of a six-week season. The vertical slice is the last three days of a week: day 5, Trial day, Games day.

Every slot is a trade: a stat, a person, or a scheme.Training raises a stat that changes an arena number. Socialising moves trust, which unlocks spell lessons, warnings and the Breaking. A scheme spends the evening on someone else's Games. The three never fit in one day.

Loop 2 of 4

The arena fight loop

A high top-down view (the "diablo view"), one thumb moving, one thumb casting. Three threat families, three answers, one shared resource tension (mana pays for both spells and the ward).

Absorb eats magic. Roll beats steel. Nothing stops the black-and-red.Magic glows in its element's colour and is absorbed with mana. Physical attacks are white and hard-edged and are rolled with stamina. Unblockable spells have a black core and a jagged red rim: leave the area, or break the caster with a staff strike.

The second-to-second loop of a bout. Orange is the player's hands; the collar clock is the clock every combatant carries.

The collar clock: tiers over time, faster with perfect absorbs

Every combatant carries its own clock; the clock resets each wave. Scale: 12 px per second. Numbers authored (combat.json → tierClock).

Tier IV is a reward for reading the enemy, not for waiting.The registry's pacing bands call a fight past 45 s spongy. A duel is authored at 45-80 s, so the base clock barely reaches tier IV. Players who land perfect absorbs get there inside the fight; players who don't, mostly won't. The clock and the parry are one mechanic.

The absorb, as a timeline

Two axes, kept separate (registry: active defence has two axes). The window is the same for everyone and never grows with a stat; Nerve changes the magnitude (drain per second, mana returned). Holding the ward through a stream gives only normal absorbs, so perfects cannot be farmed.

Loop 3 of 4

The progression loop: stats, spells, reputation, tiers

Every camp quantity has exactly one arena consequence, and every arena result writes back into the camp. Nothing in the arena is bought; it is trained, learned from someone, or earned in front of the crowd.

Green arrows run camp → arena, orange arrows arena → camp. The rank ladder (Tiro → Veteranus → Primus → Summa) is the spine.

Loop 4 of 4 · the part that must never break

The reconciliation loop: the Night Ledger and the Crier

At night the camp is reconciled in nine fixed steps. Steps 1-7 and 9 are deterministic code with seeded dice. Step 8 is the only step a language model may touch, and it touches words.

Swimlanes: what the player did, what code decides, what a model may write, what is stored. The purple lane has a fallback at every arrow.

PropertyHow it is guaranteed
Bounded14 activities, 15 goals, 4 situation machines with a closed predicate vocabulary. Caps: 2 schemes a night (3 on the eve), 1 defection a night, trust swing ≤ 40 a night, no deaths, no magic in camp.
In characterEach character's utility is weighted by its own traits and values; school biases multiply activity weights; goals change only through the trigger table. The Crier must write within a voice card and its "never" list.
ConsistentOne authority per quantity in CampState. Every fact names actors who exist and were there. A friend (trust ≥ 20) disbelieves a rumour about you.
Choices matterTested: 200 seed pairs that differ only in the player's day-1 choice must diverge by ≥ 3 board facts by day 7 in 80% of pairs.
CachedThe phrase bank is the cache: ~3,000 validated lines generated at build time by the local 27B model, keyed by fact type × voice × tone.
ValidatedSchema, cited facts exist and are not secret, no unknown names or numbers, rumours hedged, voice "never" lists, length. A failed item is replaced from the bank.
FallbackThe bank is the default. Live voice is an optional upgrade with a 4 s cut-off after the night scene.
CostDefault: 0. Live voice: 1 call a night, ~2,200 in / 450 out tokens ≈ 0.002 USD a night at placeholder prices (0.50 / 2.00 USD per million).
A friend does not believe a rumour about you.One small rule (trust ≥ 20 means no reaction) does more for "the world stays sensible" than any validator: rumours hurt you where you are weak and bounce where you are loved, which is how the player learns the camp has memory.

Systems as data

Relationships and loyalty

Four numbers carry the social world, each with one owner in CampState: directed trust (−100..100), directed debt (a count of favours owed), loyalty of a character to their tent (0..100), and hunger (0..100). Tents hold stores (bread) and a standing from the Games.

// CampState (Kotlin, in core; one authority per quantity)
class CampState(
  val trust:   DirectedMatrix,   // -100..100, clamped; swing per night <= 40
  val debt:    DirectedMatrix,   // favours owed; help() adds 1; repay_debt goal spends it
  val loyalty: Map,     // to own tent; hunger >= 50 at night: -5
  val hunger:  Map,     // +15 a night if unfed; Strays are never fed
  val renown:  Map,     // 0..100, from Games and rumours
  val tents:   Map,    // members, elder, stores, standing
  val situations: Map,
  val knowings: Map> )  // who knows what: a warning raises a scheme's dc by 2

// the defection rule (one per night, at dawn)
defects(x, toTent) = loyalty(x) < 20
                  && trust(x, m) >= 20 for some m in toTent
                  && (warmth(elder(toTent)) >= 6 || stores(toTent) >= 6)
Same rank, same tent: rivals. Same rank, other tent: friends.The owner's "duality of tribalism" is two rules: same-tent, same-rank pairs lose 1 trust a night because they compete for one Trial slot; cross-tent, equal-rank pairs gain 50% more at the Commons. The system pushes friendships outward and rivalries inward, then the Games draw friends against each other.

Systems as data · the owner's four archetypes, designed

The four archetype situations as state machines

Each archetype is a small state machine in situations.json, evaluated every night in a fixed order (a, b, c, d). Edges use a closed predicate vocabulary; an unknown predicate fails the build. The player can push every machine, and every machine moves without the player.

Systems as data

Quests: decision text with visible odds

Quests are short D&D-style graphs: a few nodes, choices gated by a stat check with the odds shown, authored endings with effects on real quantities. They are written at build time (a model helps draft text; a person or a linter approves), and the simulation instantiates them: a sabotage quest's target is whichever tent leads the standing that week.

World

Story spine and endings

The setting. A frontier legion uses collared mage auxiliaries. After the Ford is won, the wardens lock the collars and push the four through the Janus Door into Castra Clausa, a valley-world where the sun never moves. Magic dies at the collar inside the camp; it loosens only in the arena, rune by rune. Old Quill knows why: the arena's Wardstones drink the magic spilled in the Games, and that drink is what keeps the Door shut. Rome needs the fights to escalate. The collar clock is a harvest.

Six weeks, four acts, four endings. Every ending is computed from state; none is chosen from a menu.

Build

Platform and controls

Fire TV Stick 4K + an Android phone as the controller, over the LAN WebSocket the TV serves (the Death Ride stack, measured smooth on the reference Stick). A Bluetooth gamepad paired to the Stick is a first-class alternative. Kotlin + libGDX; a pure-JVM core holds both the arena kernel and the camp simulation, so both are tested headless on the desktop and run identically on the Stick.

The phone in landscape. The left thumb never leaves the stick; the right thumb lives between Bolt, Absorb and Roll, and reaches up for the four slots. Eyes stay on the TV.

Latency is designed for, not hoped away. Death Ride measured the TV + phone path; this game puts its finest timing (the perfect absorb) on it. So: every input carries a clock-corrected timestamp, the TV judges the perfect window by that timestamp up to 0.10 s late, and a hit's damage is resolved 0.10 s after its visual impact. W1 measures input age on the Stick; if p95 is above 150 ms, the gamepad becomes the default and the window is re-derived.

What the TV shows: the arena, big telegraphs in the three-family language, the collar's four runes as a ring at the mage's feet (readable from the sofa), HP and mana as two arcs. What the phone shows: buttons, cooldown rings, the slot runes, a haptic double pulse on a perfect absorb, and in the camp the slot choices and dialogue cards, so the TV stays a picture.
