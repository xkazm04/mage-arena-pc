# The broken oath

The Covenant's inscriptions are a semantic alphabet of twelve radicals, not
a substitution font. They inherit the wardstones' broken bowls, the portraits'
weathered silver collars and the UI kit's hooked material edges. Use the actual
painted cutouts in [glyphs.json](glyphs.json), shown on [the glyph sheet](glyph-sheet.png).
Do not substitute a font, a clean line drawing or another generated alphabet.

| Radical | Meaning | Distinguishing painted form |
|---|---|---|
| fire | change through heat | three uneven flame tongues, joined root |
| water | receive and return | breaking cup-wave and detached tear |
| earth | hold a place | cleft mineral tooth with two root ends |
| air | carry and divide | three swept wisps and a loose breath nick |
| bind | keep within | seed husk holding a torn dark opening |
| open | permit passage | cracked bowl and escaping tip |
| gather | draw inward | three splinters entering a cupped stroke |
| release | let energy depart | unwinding curl and outward fragments |
| witness | attend to a place | broken watchful eye and offset scars |
| danger | harm is approaching | three unequal falling splinters |
| sever | binding cannot hold | split shell with a jagged gap |
| endure | remain through force | tangled root and one rising shoot |

These are original generated paintings with a hand-drawn treatment, not a claim
that a human drew them. The source's letter-like warning cell is excluded.
`danger` is an authored ligature made from the disconnected painted splinters
of `gather`, turned downward. No procedural line supplies a visible glyph.

Construction reads clockwise from the top. A casting sentence repeats
**element → gather → bind → element → open → release → element → endure**.
The element is repeated so it survives partial occlusion by the caster. Keep
the centre clear. Glyphs follow the tangent; their hooked tips face outward.
Connecting pigment is broad, uneven and cracked, with deliberate gaps. Do not
rotate the whole inscription for idle motion: light travels through the strokes.
Start ignites that sentence, hold lets its light breathe, release erases the
binding. The effect disappears when its game-owned event ends.

The collar uses **bind → endure → witness → open**, enclosing the wearer.
A wardstone stacks **bind / witness / endure / bind** down its face. Floor
sentences alternate binding words and the four elements: **bind, fire, endure,
water, witness, earth, open, air**. Floor pigment is quieter than combat; dormant
architecture must never resemble an urgent threat. Existing portrait, UI and
A12 files remain untouched; these are replacement inscription assets for their
next integration, not retroactive edits to approved paintings.

Threat boundaries inlay the attacking element and warning splinters. Every
enemy threat has an independent ivory warning mark. **UNBLOCKABLE** adds the
severed binding, paired teeth and a double beat; this survives a greyscale view
and must never be inferred from colour alone. Fire is ember-orange, Water cyan,
Earth ochre-gold, Air lavender, matching `style-covenant.json`. The common warning
keeps its ivory/carmine colours when a school is tinted. Positive fill always
means time advancing toward impact. At zero charge the complete perimeter is
already present; brightness travels through paint and glyphs rather than hiding
the still-dangerous boundary.

The ward carries **bind, endure, gather, bind, endure, open** along its forward
half. `absorb.window` is the bright inner oath-ring, visible only during the
simulation's perfect window. `absorb.perfect` is a different, confirmed-success
flare. The artwork never changes the authored absorb angle or hit timing.

| Small mark | Construction |
|---|---|
| selection | outward open bowls and watchful eyes |
| target | inward witness marks and warning splinters |
| slowed | water + small bind |
| rooted | earth + small bind |
| burning | fire + small release |
| wet | water + small gather |
| shielded | bind + small endure |

Use a large primary radical and a smaller lower-right modifier for statuses.
Render them upright at screen scale, with space from the actor's silhouette.
Material wear follows strokes and breaks. Keep the dark pigment edge and selected
hot cuts; never add a uniform grain overlay or an all-over bloom wash.
